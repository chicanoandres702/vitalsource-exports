Chapter 20

Arts of the Pacific and of the Americas

In this chapter, you will learn to

  1. LO1 explain the art of Pacific cultures, and
  2. LO2 describe the art and architecture of the diverse cultures of the Americas.

Continuing eastward around the world, we come to two regions that together cover almost half the globe: the vast Pacific and the Americas. We began the previous chapter by stressing the contacts that had linked India and China to the evolving Mediterranean world since ancient times. Here, we might do the opposite. From the end of the last Ice Age around 10,000 years ago, when rising waters submerged the land bridge that once linked Asia and Alaska, contact between Europe, Africa, and Asia on the one hand and the Americas and the Pacific Islands on the other was largely cut off.

In Chapter 16, there is a sign of the moment when one-half of the world rediscovered the other. If you look again at Hans Holbein’s The Ambassadors (see 16.17), painted in 1533, you will notice a globe on the lower shelf. Globes became popular in the early 16th century, spurred by Columbus’s 1492 accidental encounter of lands across the Atlantic Ocean and Vasco da Gama’s 1498 discovery of a sailing route around Africa to reach India. Holbein’s globe is placed so that Europe is facing us. If you were to turn it around, you would see an emerging idea of the rest of the world. In this chapter, we fill in Holbein’s map by looking at art from the cultures of the Pacific Islands and the Americas.
